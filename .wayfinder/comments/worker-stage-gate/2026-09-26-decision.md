# Decision: gate for building the worker-thread stage

2026-09-26. **Self-adopted, awaiting user confirmation.**

Evidence:
- the [latency baseline](../../research/ts-latency-baseline.md): 63 ms for
  the quote reparse in the 800 KB Python fixture, 26 ms in TypeScript,
  4-10 ms at 50-240 KB;
- the [halt and resume contract](../ts-halt-resume-contract/2026-09-25-resolution.md):
  giant tokens and end-of-file balancing run without a callback, for
  example 78 ms and 8.3 ms;
- the [idle-slice decision](../idle-slice-continuation/2026-09-26-decision.md):
  every edit restarts the parse, and a staleness deadline bounds it.

## What a worker would fix, and what it would not

A worker thread is worth building only for a cost that stage 2 cannot
remove.

- **Fixed by a worker:**
  - **Overruns**: slices that run past the budget because tree-sitter does
    not call back (giant tokens, end-of-file balancing). On the Lisp
    thread an overrun blocks input; on a worker it does not.
  - **Parse progress while Lisp is busy but not idle**: long commands,
    process filters such as LSP traffic, GC. Idle slices make no progress
    then.
- **Not fixed by a worker:**
  - **Restarts under continuous typing.** Every edit resets the parse on
    any thread. Between keystrokes, a worker gains only the few
    milliseconds that command and redisplay take. The staleness-deadline
    hitch stays either way.
  - **Waiters** (RET under `electric-indent-mode`, `syntax-ppss` from
    commands). They need the finished tree before they can continue.

## The gate

Build the worker stage if **any** of these holds after stage 2 ships.
They are measured by stage-2 stats over at least one day of the user's
normal editing, plus the latency scenarios.

1. **Overruns hurt.** More than 10 slices a day take longer than 8 ms,
   one frame at 120 Hz.
2. **Busy Lisp leaves faces stale.** Excluding parses that were
   restarted, the 95th percentile of pending age at completion is above
   250 ms.
3. **The user notices.** The user reports stutter or lasting stale
   faces in normal editing, and the stats attribute it to 1 or 2.

If none holds, **close the map with stage 2 as the end state**. The
worker-stage fog then moves to the map's Out of scope list, as a
possible future effort with these numbers attached.

The staleness deadline's firing rate is reported but is **not** a worker
trigger. If it fires more than once per minute of typing, that calls for
a different fix, such as a parse that is not restarted from scratch or a
smaller edit region. That becomes a new fog entry, not the worker stage.

## Changes to other tickets

Stage 2 (ticket 27) must expose these stats, and its gate must include a
day of the user's editing with them recorded:
- slice count and duration histogram, with the maximum;
- pending age at completion, split into restarted and not restarted;
- restarts per completed parse;
- the number of times the staleness deadline fired.
