## What was built

- `src/treesit_budget.c` parses through `ts_parser_parse_with_options`
  (ABI 15 and later) with a progress callback that never halts, and
  plain `ts_parser_parse` below ABI 15. MS-Windows loads tree-sitter
  dynamically, so there `treesit_budget_parse` is a macro for
  `ts_parser_parse` and the stats stay zero.
- `treesit-budget-stats` (optional RESET) returns a plist:
  - `:parses`, all parses;
  - `:first-parses`, `:first-seconds` and `:first-max-seconds`, for
    parses with no previous tree (file visits);
  - `:seconds`, `:max-seconds`, `:over-1ms`, `:over-3ms` and
    `:over-8ms`, for reparses only;
  - `:progress-calls`.

  First parses are kept apart because they stay synchronous in stage 2
  and would otherwise swamp the over-8 ms count.
- The hooks follow [Keep the change small in upstream files](../sync-surface-containment/2026-09-26-decision.md):
  one call in `treesit_ensure_parsed`, one line in `syms_of_treesit`,
  the declarations in `treesit.h` and the object in `Makefile.in`.
  `AGENTS.md` lists them. The parser struct field and the
  `treesit_delete_parser` line are left for stage 2, which first needs
  per-parser state.
- Tests: `test/src/treesit_budget-tests.el` (4 tests). Its name follows
  the test Makefile's rule, which maps `src/FOO-tests.el` to `src/FOO.c`.

## A 600 s reparse from an old TypeScript grammar

A replay of typing edits on the user's files (`ts-replay.el`) found one
reparse of about 600 s: a `"` typed at the start of a line in a
TypeScript file. Everything else stayed under 50 ms (p99 0.35 ms).

- The previous build (`Emacs-ba7e776.app`) takes the same time
  (606 s against 605.5 s), so stage 1 did not cause it.
- The cause is the grammar. The user's `libtree-sitter-typescript.dylib`
  was built in October 2023 (ABI 14). With it, a parse from scratch of
  the edited text takes as long (605 s) as the reparse. The current upstream
  grammar (`75b3874`, January 2025, also ABI 14) parses from scratch
  in 0.4 ms and reparses in 0.2 ms. Runtime: tree-sitter 0.27.0.
- A `sample` shows the time spent in `ts_parser_parse` calling the
  grammar's `ts_lex` over and over.

For stage 2: a grammar pathology like this is what the budget must
survive. Test whether the progress callback fires inside that loop,
so a halt can take effect. Continuing in idle slices would
still spend the 600 s of CPU, so a parse that runs this long needs a
cap as well as the budget.
